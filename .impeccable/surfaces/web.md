# Surface brief: web (the landing, the app, the tour)

Written 2026-11-02 for the design retrofit (hub T-1007). Product truth is in `PRODUCT.md`; this file holds
the strategy for this surface. Assumptions are listed under Open decisions, each with the reading taken.

## Scope and mode

One web product, three regions, one mode each:

| Region | Mode | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| The landing (Home stage) and the guided tour | Persuade | The visitor understands in one line what Strata is and presses the one button |
| The 3D scene | Experience | The bridge leads; the interface recedes; nothing covers the subject |
| The panel, dock, controls and docent | Operate | A visitor finds a part, a year, a source and an answer without a manual |

This pass is a retrofit: the information architecture and app layout stay. The direction changes palette,
type, the material of panels and chrome, radii, the landing's composition and the voice.

## Audience

Visitors, students and teachers, on a laptop or a phone, in daylight, with a few minutes. A judge or a
reviewer at a table on a laptop, who has seen 3D-landmark projects before and decides in one look.

## Job

See how a landmark was built and how it works, not read about it: watch the bridge rise 1886 to 1894,
lift the bascules, tap a part, and get a number with its source.

## Action

One big button on the first screen: **Take the guided tour**. It runs the main journey end to end:
orbit, the year slider back to 1886 and forward, exploded view, the lift, a part with its sources, the
docent, then hands the controls back. A second, quieter action: **Enter Tower Bridge** (skip straight to
the scene). Nothing else competes on the first screen.

## Proof

Real material only, already on hand: the live render of the bridge; the 1890 steel frame on the 1893 to
1896 OS map; the LiDAR point cloud coloured by height; ten archive photographs; the inspector's figures
with status and source (65 m tower, text-attested; bascules to 86 degrees); the docent's cited answer and
its refusal. Numbers the first screen may carry, each from the README: 1 m LiDAR, 1.5 km of ground,
1886 to 1894, 25 parts, 14 events, 24 sources, 65 m, 86 degrees.

## Constraints

- The scene is never covered on either size (1280x633, 390x757); a sheet or card over the bridge fails.
- Phones: one column, 16 px body, 44 px targets, the bottom sheet collapsed on first load.
- Attributions stay on screen where the data shows (LiDAR, NLS map, OSM, Google tiles, Esri).
- Reduced motion respected; one authored moment per view.
- No inline colour, size, radius or shadow in components once tokens exist; every value from `tokens.css`.
- Other builders own the tour's card, labels and controls layout, and the terrain: the direction restyles
  them through tokens, it does not move them.

## Journeys (at most three)

1. **First visit.** Landing → Take the guided tour → the tour's seven steps → hand-off ("Now you try: tap
   any part") → the visitor taps a part and reads a figure with its source.
2. **Returning or curious.** Landing → Enter Tower Bridge → drag the year to 1890 → exploded → raise the
   bascules → ask the docent a question.
3. **On the spot.** Phone, standing by the bridge → Identify a photo → the parts named and boxed → tap one
   → the inspector.

## Information hierarchy

Landing: the bridge still (largest), the name and one line, the one button, then the real numbers, then
the places coming next (smallest, one line each, no cards). App: the scene; the dock (year, event, bascule);
the panel (place, search, parts, inspector, docent); the controls (view, exploded, LiDAR, See it in,
Identify); the attribution line. Tour card: caption, step title, Next, Back, Skip, dots.

## The first screen

Desktop: the still of the bridge takes more than half the width; left or over it, "Strata", the one line
("History is text and flat photos. Strata lets you watch Tower Bridge being built, from real LiDAR and its
own sourced history."), the button **Take the guided tour**, the link Enter Tower Bridge, and the real
numbers in the direction's own form (a dimension string, a legend, a gauge). Phone: the still in a clear
band above the fold, the line and the button under it, numbers below; no card over the bridge.

## States as direction

- **Loading:** the scene's first frame under a short line ("Loading the LiDAR ground, 1.5 km of it") in
  the panel's style; never a spinner alone; the dock and controls stay put.
- **Empty (no part selected):** the inspector shows what tapping does ("Tap any part of the bridge, or pick
  one above. Each figure shows its source and status."), never a blank heading.
- **Error (API away, no key):** the docent says what it cannot do and what still works ("The docent is
  offline. The parts, sources and timeline still work."); search falls back to names and says so.
- **Refusal:** the docent's refusal is a normal, calm message with the same chip style as a citation.
- **No WebGL:** the fallback card with the film link, in the same chrome.

## Vocabulary (one name per thing)

place, part, source, status (text-attested, measured, inferred), event, year, timeline, docent, bascule,
lift, exploded view, LiDAR, top-down, walk, exhibit, guided tour, the 60 s story. Buttons say what happens:
Take the guided tour, Enter Tower Bridge, Play the 60 s story, Raise the bascules, Ask, See it in 1890,
Identify a photo. Sentence case everywhere. Never "explore", "discover", "immersive", "journey" in copy.

## The guided tour, in story order

The problem first, the wow on screen by 10 s, the problem, the wow and one number landed by 20 s.

| # | Step | Caption (the visitor sees) | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The problem | "History is text and flat photos. This is Tower Bridge on the ground the Environment Agency measured, 1 m at a time." | 0 to 6 s, the bridge in orbit |
| 2 | Watch it being built | The year runs 1886 to 1894: piers, steel frames, stone. "Eight years, stage by stage, each with its source." | wow by 10 s |
| 3 | Watch it work | Signals to red, the leaves rise to 86 degrees on simulated hydraulics, the ship comes through. "Driven by a simulation, not a clip." | the number by 20 s |
| 4 | Take it apart | Exploded view. "Every part pulls away; tap one." | |
| 5 | Tap any part | The north tower: 65 m, text-attested, its source. "Every number has a source and a status." | |
| 6 | Ask the docent | A live answer with citation chips; "and it says so when it cannot cite one." | |
| 7 | The ground | Top-down, LiDAR point cloud. "The real ground, coloured by height." | |
| 8 | Now you try | Hand-off: "Tap any part." Skip is visible at every step. | |

Identify, Reimagine, voice, search and walk are not tour steps; they are discovered from the controls.

## Open decisions (asserted, correct me)

| Question shape's discovery would ask | Reading taken |
|---|---|
| Is the landing a separate page or the first state of the app? | The first state of the app (the Home stage), so the direction's chrome carries straight through |
| Does the light theme stay? | No commitment; a hackathon-day restyle nobody reviewed. Light or dark is chosen from the use scene by the direction and named in its contract |
| Which numbers may the first screen show? | Only those in the README with a source behind them; no visitor counts, no invented stats |
| Is the tour the main action or the scene? | The tour: it runs the whole journey and the judges saw too little of the key feature last time (lesson 20) |
| Are the coming places cards? | No; one line each, no cards, no "Soon" chips |
| Does the direction touch the 3D materials? | No; the terrain and bridge are owned elsewhere. The direction may set the page under the canvas, the labels' type and the colour grade's framing only through tokens |

## Direction contract

**D2 Survey: the ground as data.** Chosen by the blind judge (ranked first, R1 1 against Felt, R2 2, one
tell, 18/24) and by the design pass; the full verdict is in the hub project folder. The tokens in this
file's frontmatter are normative; `web/src/tokens.css` is generated from them and never hand-edited.

- **Mechanism.** The LiDAR ground is the hero. Every figure carries its status as a dot and a word
  (measured, text-attested, inferred); the year slider is a measured scale with a tick every decade; a
  height legend runs the full height of the still's edge. Panels are matte paper with one hairline.
- **Memorable.** The status dot and word on every figure, and the height ramp on the real point cloud.
- **Theme.** Light only (`themes: [light]`): the scene is a daylight survey, and the judge scored the
  light ground as matching the use scene. No dark mode.
- **Colour.** Seed `#A67C00`, survey ochre, harmony complementary, so the neutrals are a cool instrument
  grey. The accent goes only to the one action (Take the guided tour, and the primary control of each
  stage) and to the live year mark. Status roles: `status-measured` is `success`, `status-text-attested`
  is `info`, `status-inferred` is `muted`; a status always shows its word beside the dot. `warning` sits
  near the accent's hue, so a warning always carries an icon and a label. The height ramp (deep blue to
  teal to ochre) is the one gradient and appears only where it encodes height: never on a button, a
  heading, a background or a divider.
- **Type.** Display Geologica 600, body Public Sans 400 and 600, both self-hosted from
  `@fontsource-variable/geologica` and `@fontsource-variable/public-sans`; base 16, ratio 1.25; figures
  tabular (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) everywhere a number can change.
- **Shape and space.** Radii: control 4, panel 8, pill 999 only for status chips. Spacing 4, 8, 12, 16,
  24, 32. Controls 44 px tall or more.
- **Material over the live scene.** The panel, the dock and the docent are opaque `surface` with a
  `border` hairline: no blur, no glass, no shadow. There is no scrim; `scrim` is the same opaque surface
  for the rare full-cover state (no WebGL, loading). Nothing semi-transparent sits over the bridge.
- **Placement (the judge's B8 fixes, now rules).** At 1280 the panel and the dock are page chrome, not
  scene chrome: the panel is a side sheet on the page ground beside the canvas and the dock is a strip
  below it, so neither sits on the subject. Attribution sits in the page chrome under the canvas or
  still, never on the picture. At 390 the attribution chip sits below the still band, not inside it, and
  nothing covers the subject inside the band. The 3D canvas keeps a clear subject at both sizes.
- **Motion.** One moment: on load the still crosses from the aerial drape to the point cloud (opacity,
  900 ms, ease-out); the year mark slides when the year changes. Everything else answers with a colour
  change. All of it off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Leaves out.** Glass, blur, shadows, gradients other than the ramp, serif, filled chips, dark mode, a
  stat-tile row, cards over the scene, eyebrow labels, "coming next" cards.
- **One hard limit.** The ramp appears only where it encodes height.

## Voice

Read by visitors to one place and the judges watching them. Nouns: place, part, source, status, year,
event, docent, bascule, lift.
- Do: Strata rebuilds Tower Bridge on the ground the Environment Agency measured, 1 m at a time, and runs the years 1886 to 1894 stage by stage.
- Don't: Strata is an immersive, AI-powered journey through history like never before.
- Do: North tower, 65 m, text-attested. Source: Tower Bridge, the official history.
- Don't: The iconic tower soars an incredible 65 m into the London sky.
- Do: The docent has no source for that, so it will not answer it. Try a part, a year or an event.
- Don't: Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later.
